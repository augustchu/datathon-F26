# Direction 1 — Recover missing fields from attributable source evidence

**Why this is a good direction.** Some missing structured values are already present in facility descriptions, source pages, and accreditation scope documents. Recovering those facts can improve the dataset while giving the competition exactly what it asks for: an explanation tied to underlying facility text. The most promising extension is a branch-specific extraction and review layer for capacity, doctor counts, equipment, and explicitly named services. It should preserve the quote, date, unit, scope, and contradictory evidence for every recovered value. This builds directly on the team's description-based capacity experiment and existing verification pipeline. Its value should be measured by correct new facts and better-supported recommendations, even when retraining does not improve prediction error.

**Review date:** 21 November 2026, America/New_York. The original literature review is targeted. The deterministic pilot, attribution and review pipeline, and reproducible sampling workflow are now documented in the [implementation and decision log](../../non-ml/SOURCE_GROUNDED_FIELD_RECOVERY.md). The comparative research design below describes the proposed next evaluation stage.

## Fit with the prompt and the team's current work

The supplied competition prompt asks for a service and a location, followed by facility options that explain both **why this facility** and **how the decision was made**. It specifically warns that records can merge hospitals and contain unsupported service claims. A recovered value therefore needs to answer two different questions: does the passage support the value, and does the passage refer to this exact branch?

The current [project summary](../../non-ml/PROJECT_SUMMARY.md) records 10,088 Virtue Foundation rows and 51 fields, with 2,509 accepted exact bed counts among 10,073 linked branches. The [source comparison](../../non-ml/DATA_COMPARISON.md) shows useful complementary service evidence in government listings, NABH scope documents, and blood-bank directories. The current [reviewed evidence registry](../../non-ml/config/reviewed_service_evidence.json) demonstrates the intended pattern: an exact branch, a short service excerpt, a publisher, and a dated or scoped source.

The latest **working-tree** [model evaluation](../../ML/MODEL_EVALUATION.md) reports that a description pass recovered 12 previously blank whole-facility capacity counts; nine belonged to the training partition. Among 183 branches with both an extracted and structured value, 180 agreed. That is agreement between fields in the same dataset, not independent verification. Adding the nine training examples produced no consistent model improvement. This is evidence for a useful recovery mechanism with limited current yield, rather than evidence that large-scale automatic label expansion will work.

The user's later request to use all relevant available information and populate capacity from descriptions makes this a direct continuation. It also preserves the three comparison modes: reviewed source claims can enter the documented-data layer, while predictions remain visibly estimated. It produces useful inputs for the teammate's load-balancing work without claiming that bed totals establish free appointments or current occupancy.

## What the academic literature contributes

Searches covered primary conference and journal sources for structured extraction, citation attribution, entity matching, and weak supervision, including 2025–2026 work and foundational methods. The findings below distinguish published results from the proposed adaptation to this project. None of these papers validates Indian facility-service recommendations.

| Study and publication | Finding relevant to the project | Design implication and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gao, Yen, Yu and Chen (2023), **Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations**, EMNLP ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)) | ALCE evaluates factual correctness and citation quality separately. The tested systems could produce answers whose citations did not fully support them. | Require a supporting passage for each atomic recovered claim and score support separately from extraction accuracy. A real URL alone is insufficient. ALCE is a question-answering benchmark; its error rates should not be transferred to our extraction task. |
| Buchmann, Liu and Gurevych (2024), **Attribute or Abstain: Large Language Models as Long Document Assistants**, EMNLP ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.463/)) | Across six long-document tasks, attribution strategy interacted with model size and training. Evidence quality was less informative for complex answers than for simple ones. | Extract small claims such as branch–service–scope rather than a single narrative about a hospital. Permit an explicit unanswered result. This motivates testing retrieval and chunking locally; it does not establish that every apparently supported claim is correct. |
| Zaratiana et al. (2025), **GLiNER2: Schema-Driven Multi-Task Learning for Structured Information Extraction**, EMNLP System Demonstrations ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.10/)) | A compact encoder supports entity recognition, classification, and structured extraction through a schema interface, with CPU-oriented deployment. | Compare a small schema-based extractor with existing deterministic rules before introducing a large generative model. Benchmark actual memory and latency in Free Edition. Published benchmark results do not establish handling of hospital scope, multilingual pages, or long accreditation documents. |
| Sibue et al. (2026), **ExStrucTiny: A Benchmark for Schema-Variable Structured Information Extraction from Document Images**, EACL ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.265/)) | Its document-image evaluation exposes challenges in schema adaptation, underspecified requests, and locating answers; open-model performance declines as the requested value count grows. | For scanned tables and certificates, preserve page and row context and test short, explicit schemas. Do not assume accurate OCR implies correct table associations. This is a document-image benchmark, so it informs PDF evaluation rather than proving performance on our web text. |
| Li et al. (2020), **Deep Entity Matching with Pre-Trained Language Models**, PVLDB 14(1), 50–60 ([paper](https://vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p50-li.pdf)) | Ditto casts matching as sequence-pair classification and uses domain knowledge, text summarization, and augmentation to improve benchmark matching. | Branch linkage deserves its own benchmark and hard negatives, such as two hospitals with the same name. Retain the existing conservative linker as a baseline. Results on other entity domains do not justify merging hospitals without address evidence. |
| Wang et al. (2025), **Match, Compare, or Select? An Investigation of Large Language Models for Entity Matching**, COLING ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.8/)) | Across eight entity-resolution datasets and ten LLMs, considering competing records through selection improved matching; ComEM combines strategies for efficiency. | Compare plausible sibling branches together, including a “none or ambiguous” outcome. Check consistency across linked records. Unlike some clean-table matching settings, this repository can legitimately contain many source rows per branch, so do not impose universal one-to-one matching. |
| Ratner et al. (online 2019; volume 2020), **Snorkel: Rapid Training Data Creation with Weak Supervision**, The VLDB Journal 29, 709–730 ([paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00778-019-00552-1)) | Labeling functions combine noisy heuristics while accounting for disagreements and dependencies. Weak labels can train downstream models without being treated as hand-verified truth. | If simple rules plateau, combine pattern, scope, negation, and source signals as experimental labels. Keep an independently reviewed evaluation set and preserve label provenance. Several copied pages or correlated rules do not constitute independent corroboration. |

These studies support an **extract, attribute, link, review** research design. They do not support accepting a model's self-reported confidence as proof, or substituting extraction agreement for evidence of current service availability.

## Proposed extension

Keep the original rows immutable and create a long-form claim table. Each record should include `facility_id`, source record ID, field, raw value, normalized value, unit, scope, stance, source URL, excerpt, text offsets or PDF page, document date where available, retrieval date, publisher family, extraction method/version, linkage basis, and review status. Retrieval date must not be presented as the date the underlying fact became true.

Use separate statuses for source-reported, extracted-unreviewed, reviewed-source-claim, independently-corroborated, conflicting, unknown, and statistically-estimated values. A reviewed source claim means the extraction and branch attribution were checked; it does not establish real-world truth or present availability. Preserve multiple time-qualified claims instead of overwriting disagreement with whichever source was downloaded last.

Start with four deliberately narrow field definitions:

| Field | Acceptable claim | Examples requiring rejection or a separate field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole-facility beds | A branch-specific current or explicitly dated total, with the capacity basis retained | ICU-only beds, network totals, expansion plans, cumulative patients, approximate ranges represented as exact counts |
| Doctors | A documented branch count with its definition and date | Visiting roster entries counted as unique full-time doctors; network-wide staff totals |
| Equipment | Explicit equipment presence at the branch, with subtype | An MRI service inferred only from “radiology”; a procurement notice treated as installed equipment |
| Services | Explicit named procedure or service, with subtype and scope | Nephrology treated as dialysis; maternity treated as emergency C-section; historical activity treated as today's availability |

Build on [claim extraction](../../non-ml/src/facility_tool/data.py), [reviewed-claim attachment](../../non-ml/src/facility_tool/reviewed_claims.py), [service verification](../../non-ml/src/facility_tool/verification.py), and [location holds](../../non-ml/src/facility_tool/geo_audit.py). Do not release a held branch because a new description contains a plausible service. Deduplicate near-identical passages and record publisher relationships before counting corroboration.

Run extraction once per snapshot. The recommendation query should read prepared evidence and ranking inputs; it should not browse or ask a language model to reconstruct the hospital at runtime. This matches the user's preference for modular notebooks and minimal work per query.

## A bounded experiment

1. **Freeze the definitions and cases.** Begin with 60 development cases and a separately locked set of 120 distinct-facility test cases, approximately balanced across the four fields. Include simple positives, absent evidence, same-name branches, negation, conflicting dates, unit-level counts, and multi-facility pages. Deliberately include hard cases, then report this as a diagnostic sample rather than a population prevalence estimate.
2. **Create adjudicated references.** Two reviewers independently label the value, branch, scope, citation support, and answerability; resolve differences with written reasons. Retain the evidence available at the evaluation date. Report disagreement and unresolved cases instead of forcing a value. For population recovery yield, add a separate random sample of genuinely missing field records.
3. **Compare three extraction approaches.** Evaluate the existing rule-based baseline, a schema-driven small model, and an optional constrained generative extractor using exactly the same passages. First test extraction with known source passages; separately measure retrieval failures. A generative candidate must return a source span that exists in the saved document, but span existence alone does not prove entailment.
4. **Evaluate branch association independently.** Include competing branches and unresolved links. Score correct value-and-branch-and-scope tuples, not just whether a number or service name was found. Split duplicate passages and related branch records together to avoid a source appearing in both development and test.
5. **Measure downstream value.** On a frozen query set, count newly supported branch–service pairs, corrected exclusions, and answerable searches. Compare recommendations before and after review. Report recovered bed/doctor facts separately from service-eligibility changes.

Primary metrics are accepted-claim precision, answerable-case recall, abstention rate, correct tuple rate, unsupported-citation rate, wrong-branch rate, and reviewer minutes per accepted new fact. Report by field, source type, and language. For independent binary cases, include binomial uncertainty intervals; if multiple observations share a facility or source, use grouped uncertainty estimates instead. A 120-case diagnostic set cannot establish a national error rate or reliable performance for every subgroup.

**Proposed pilot advancement criteria:** at least 95% observed accepted-claim precision overall, no observed wrong-branch acceptances, and a pooled 95% interval lower bound of at least 90% where the sample supports that calculation. Require per-field inspection and retain manual approval for underpowered fields. These are project-selected research gates, not published clinical standards. If a model misses these gates or saves no review time, keep it as a candidate generator and retain deterministic extraction plus review.

## How to use recovered values in modeling

Record recovered labels separately from original structured labels. Use only eligible training-partition recoveries to augment training; never let the recovery process move a held-out branch into training. Exclude the passage that supplied a training target from that target's predictors, and audit duplicate or paraphrased target statements elsewhere. Keep document families and facility groups together when constructing a new evaluation split.

Report three separate outcomes: extraction correctness, new-label yield, and downstream imputation performance. The existing nine-label augmentation result makes this separation essential. An accurate recovery layer can be worthwhile even when it adds too few examples to improve a forest. Conversely, a better forest score does not validate a wrongly attributed source claim.

## Why prioritize this now

This direction turns the team's existing investment in messy text, public sources, branch audits, and description recovery into a demonstrable competition advantage. A judge can inspect a blank field becoming a dated, attributable claim and see how it affects a recommendation. The unresolved risk is yield: the descriptions already produced only a small set of usable missing bed totals. Expanding carefully to services, equipment, and source documents is the hypothesis to test, with retrieval cost and manual review measured explicitly. The first deliverable should be a small audited claim table and a reproducible notebook, followed by a Free Edition run, before any national-scale automatic enrichment is claimed.
