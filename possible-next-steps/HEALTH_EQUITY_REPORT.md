# Health Equity in Hospital Search in India

**Datathon research report · November 21, 2026**

**Evaluate clinical outcomes and respectful care together.** For a specific service, ask both what happened to patients’ health and how they were treated. Some Indian hospitals and studies publish actual outcomes, but the sources reviewed do not support a broad comparison of both measures by branch, service and demographic group. Start with a partner pilot that pairs clinical records with an independent patient survey. In the finder, show sourced evidence when it is available and let people compare verified practical needs such as language support, accessibility and scheme coverage.

“Best” depends on what a person needs to receive safe, effective and respectful care. A patient arranging repeated dialysis may prioritize travel cost and whether their scheme covers the treatment. A Deaf patient may need a qualified interpreter through the entire visit. A patient who has experienced mistreatment may want evidence about privacy, respectful communication or what happens when someone reports a problem. These are barriers a facility and health system can address. The standard of care should be equally high for every group. [WHO, Health equity](https://www.who.int/health-topics/health-equity/) · [WHO, Quality health services](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/quality-health-services)

## Evidence of unequal access and treatment

| Evidence from India | What the study found and what it can support |
| --- | --- |
| **Caste and education within the same facility** | An Odisha assessment interviewed 1,485 inpatients and outpatients. More-educated and higher-caste patients reported better interpersonal treatment within the same facility. Across the study, Scheduled Tribe respondents were 14 percentage points less likely than respondents from other social groups to report being treated with dignity and respect. The exit surveys captured experiences of people who obtained care. [Yip et al., *Health Systems & Reform*, 2022](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23288604.2022.2132366) |
| **Religion and caste at the point of care** | Zutshi randomly varied the presented identities of men making standardized asthma visits to 596 private providers in Bihar. Visible Muslim presentation reduced measured technical quality by 0.21 standard deviations and interpersonal quality by 0.19 standard deviations relative to the upper-caste Hindu presentation. Differences for the Dalit and Muslim-name-only presentations were statistically inconclusive. The measured effects came primarily from informal providers. [Zutshi, *Social Science & Medicine*, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42160981/) |
| **Caste and Adivasi experience** | In a mixed-methods study in Jhargram, West Bengal, 42% of Dalit households and 38% of Adivasi households reported healthcare discrimination, compared with 12% of households from other castes in the study. Participants described avoidance and reluctance to examine patients. Some described withholding caste identity. The estimates are local household reports, with experiences often described in village and primary-care settings. [Bango et al., *International Journal for Equity in Health*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12939-025-02734-6) |
| **Gender and affordability** | Dupas and Jain found large gender gaps in the use of a Rajasthan insurance scheme covering 46 million eligible people. Patients faced costs despite formal entitlement to free hospital care. Lowering costs raised female utilization without closing the gap. In a separate standardized-patient study in Mumbai and Patna, researchers detected no clear difference by presented gender in initial TB case management. The studies examine distinct steps in care and illustrate why a gender effect should be tested for a specific service and setting. [Dupas and Jain, *American Economic Review*, 2024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10198787/) · [Daniels et al., *The Lancet Global Health*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X(19)30031-2) |
| **Transgender patients and overlapping barriers** | In participatory interviews and group discussions, transgender people described gendered queues and forms, identification mismatches, disclosure risks and poor privacy in routine care. Participants described how factors including caste, language, disability and migration could compound barriers. The qualitative findings identify points in the visit to audit and inform survey wording. [Raghuram et al., *PLOS Global Public Health*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0002933) |
| **Disability and travel** | Participatory audits of 35 government hospitals in Madhya Pradesh and Goa found ramps that did not meet users’ needs and no sign-language interpreters at the audited hospitals. A separate two-district study documented barriers involving transport, examination equipment and physical access. Check the actual route from arrival to the treatment area and the accommodation available at the relevant time. [Madhya Pradesh and Goa accessibility audits, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13543994/) · [Two-district disability study, *BMC Public Health*, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2458-14-1125) |
| **Language and geography** | Research across six Indian emergency departments documented language and interpretation challenges. The national Tribal Health Expert Committee also documented geographic, staffing and communication barriers and called for culturally appropriate services. Check language support at the service and shift level, and use local travel conditions when evaluating whether an option is feasible. [Emergency department language study, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12245-021-00380-7) · [Government of India Tribal Health Expert Committee report](https://nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/Tribal_Report/Tribal_Health_Report.pdf) |

The studies distinguish three ways a directory can miss what matters. Some patients cannot reach or afford care. Others face different treatment once they arrive. People who succeed in getting care may still have trouble reporting a negative experience. The research justifies making these barriers visible and measuring them directly. The effect of adding this feature to hospital search is a separate question for the pilot.

## Where to find actual clinical outcomes

Indian sources report events such as death, infection, stroke and readmission, along with some measures of recovery. The available evidence ranges from named-facility disclosures to pooled or masked research results. Coverage is uneven, and a public aggregate can describe one hospital or cohort while leaving its demographic breakdown and patient experience unknown.

The [named-hospital maternity data audit](MATERNITY_OUTCOMES_AUDIT.md) extracts counts, denominators and dates, checks overlap with patient-reported respect, and documents which demographic breakdowns the studies published.

| Source | What it reports and how to use it |
| --- | --- |
| **Caesarean outcomes at MediCiti, Telangana** | In a prospective 2017–19 cohort at MediCiti Institute of Medical Sciences, 92 of 2,015 participants developed a surgical-site infection within 30 days. Eight of those infections were first detected after discharge. The study published its criteria, follow-up procedure and a supplementary dataset. It is a practical template for collecting a comparable measure today. [Basany et al., *PLOS ONE*, 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286165) |
| **Government audit of Lok Nayak Hospital, Delhi** | The Comptroller and Auditor General reports annual stillbirth counts and delivery denominators for 2016–17 through 2021–22, totaling 1,238 stillbirths against 60,697 deliveries. The government told the auditors that most cases were referrals from other centres. Use this as a historical outcome record and keep the referral context attached. [CAG audit, table 3.15](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2024/7_Chapter-3-Healthcare-Services-067c18f3519e939.35643486.pdf#page=31) |
| **Named-hospital surgical surveillance and disclosures** | An ICMR-funded study followed 3,090 surgical patients at three named hospitals, including AIIMS New Delhi’s trauma centre, and published surgical-site infection rates with counts and confidence intervals. The cohort spans multiple procedures and historical periods. Fortis Mohali also publishes observed mortality, stroke and deep sternal wound infection after heart bypass surgery. The Fortis table is provider-reported and the main table lacks case counts and a clearly stated reporting window. [ICMR-funded surgical surveillance study, 2024](https://ijmr.org.in/surveillance-for-surgical-site-infections-developed-during-hospital-stay-after-discharge-a-multicentric-study/) · [Fortis Mohali bypass outcomes](https://www.fortishealthcare.com/clinical-outcomes/coronary-artery-bypass-graft-cabg-2) |
| **Haemodialysis network research** | Hockham et al. studied 23,601 patients across 193 NephroPlus centres. Adjusted estimates of survival 180 days into follow-up ranged from 83% to 97% across centres. Follow-up began 90 days after joining a centre. The historical centre estimates are anonymized, and early deaths and losses to follow-up affect interpretation. In 2026, a parliamentary committee called for the national dialysis programme to add outcomes including mortality and treatment discontinuation to its reporting. [Hockham et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38601176/) · [Parliamentary committee recommendations, §5.6.6](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2296281&lang=2&reg=48) |
| **LaQshya maternity reports** | The government reporting template already specifies monthly facility data on deliveries, stillbirths, neonatal deaths and surgical-site infections within one month of a caesarean. It also includes a satisfaction item. Ask a state National Health Mission or hospital partner about an authorized export, register completeness and follow-up, and collect a dedicated measure of respect. [LaQshya Annexure 10, facility reporting format and registers](https://nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/RMNCH_MH_Guidelines/LaQshya-Guidance-note-Annexures.pdf) |
| **PM-JAY and disease registries** | The National Health Authority’s performance framework lists readmissions, patient satisfaction and improvement in health-related quality of life. The ICMR hospital-based stroke registry collects outcomes, and a joint NHA–ICMR report uses PM-JAY claims to describe in-hospital stroke mortality, with age and sex among the available variables. These are leads for authorized data access and service-specific benchmarks. Comparable branch-level public exports with both clinical outcomes and respectful care were unverified in this review. [NHA performance framework](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1889730&lang=2&reg=48) · [ICMR National Stroke Registry](https://stroke.ncdirindia.org/) · [NHA–ICMR PM-JAY stroke outcomes report](https://www.ncdirindia.org/All_Reports/Stroke/POSC_ABPM_JAY.pdf) |

Named-facility subgroup studies also exist. At Kasturba Hospital’s newborn unit in Jhagadia, Gujarat, mortality during admission was 10.5% for newborns the study classified as tribal and 5.9% for the comparison group in its 2013–21 cohort. The estimated odds ratio fell from 1.86 to 1.12 after adjusting for birth weight, gestational age and whether the newborn was born at the hospital. The adjusted 95% interval (0.89–1.42) included equal odds. This illustrates how much the interpretation can depend on the patients each group includes and the outcomes a hospital tracks. The study classified identity by surname and tracked outcomes through the end of the unit stay. A Datathon pilot would use optional self-identification and track what happens after transfer where feasible. [Lusk et al., *Indian Journal of Medical Research*, 2024, table II](https://ijmr.org.in/characteristics-outcomes-of-tribal-non-tribal-neonates-admitted-to-a-special-newborn-care-unit-in-rural-gujarat-india/)

Indian studies show that both kinds of information can be collected in the same cohort. Raj et al. surveyed 2,639 patients who gave birth at 68 Uttar Pradesh public facilities. Patients reporting mistreatment had higher odds of reporting postpartum complications (adjusted odds ratio 2.12, 95% CI 1.67–2.68). Both measures were self-reported in a cross-sectional survey, so the result is an association. The BetterBirth trial tracked deaths and severe complications at 120 facilities and asked two general satisfaction questions during the same follow-up when the mother and newborn survived. That approach demonstrates feasibility and illustrates how eligibility for an experience survey can systematically miss families with adverse outcomes. The trial improved adherence to essential practices, with no detectable change in its primary clinical outcome. [Raj et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28676965/) · [BetterBirth trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa1701075) · [BetterBirth follow-up methods](https://betterbirth.ariadnelabs.org/appendix-b-summary-of-data-sources/) · [BetterBirth experience analysis](https://betterbirth.ariadnelabs.org/women-community-power-matters/)

## What to measure

Measure clinical outcomes and respectful care at the **exact branch, service and reporting period**. Alongside them, show capabilities and conditions for access, such as equipment or an interpreter. Accreditation and service records can help establish those capabilities. Patient outcomes come from a defined cohort with documented events and follow-up, while respectful treatment can be measured with private patient feedback. [WHO Harmonized Health Facility Assessment](https://www.who.int/data/data-collection-tools/harmonized-health-facility-assessment/introduction) · [WHO standards for maternal and newborn care](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241511216) · [NHSRC National Quality Assurance Standards](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/national-quality-assurance-standards)

| Factor | Candidate indicators and how to obtain them |
| --- | --- |
| **Clinical outcomes for the requested service** | Prespecify one outcome and its eligibility rules and time window, such as surgical-site infection within 30 days of a caesarean. Count eligible procedures, confirmed events, transfers and unknown outcomes. Check records and follow-up after discharge using a consistent surveillance method. For dialysis, a network could instead measure survival from treatment initiation or bloodstream infections per catheter-days using active surveillance. [LaQshya reporting fields](https://nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/RMNCH_MH_Guidelines/LaQshya-Guidance-note-Annexures.pdf) · [Surgical infection surveillance study](https://ijmr.org.in/surveillance-for-surgical-site-infections-developed-during-hospital-stay-after-discharge-a-multicentric-study/) · [Dialysis survival cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38601176/) |
| **Physical and sensory accessibility** | Step-free path from drop-off to reception, treatment room and toilets, suitable lifts and exam equipment, accessible signage and appointment channels. Record the audit date, service area and whether the auditor tested the route with patients who use it. [MoHFW Accessibility Standards for Healthcare](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Accessibility_Standard_English_merged.pdf) |
| **Language and communication** | Languages available for registration, consultation and consent, qualified interpreter availability and how to book one, accessible written or audio instructions. Confirm coverage for the requested service and hours. Ask patients whether explanations were given in a form and language they understood. [India emergency department language study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12245-021-00380-7) |
| **Affordability and admission** | Current scheme and service-package participation, fees for drugs and tests, deposits, waiting time, referrals, appointments patients could obtain and reasons people were turned away. Verify formal policies against beneficiary experience and receipts where practical. [Official PM-JAY hospital lookup](https://janaushadhi.gov.in/pm-hospital) · [Dupas and Jain, 2024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10198787/) |
| **Respect, privacy and agency** | Whether staff listened, explained choices, sought consent before an examination, respected privacy, used the patient’s chosen name where appropriate and enabled a safe complaint. Record reported unfair treatment and its reason as a separate item. Audit private registration, written grievance procedures and whether a usable escalation channel exists. [NQAS 2024, Patient Rights, Area B](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2024-05/National%20Quality%20Assurance%20Standards%202024%20_revised.pdf) · [Raghuram et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0002933) |
| **Travel and practical access** | Reachable route and travel time by the patient’s mode, recurring travel costs, appointment hours, referral steps and accommodations for a caregiver. District data can help identify areas to study. Confirm the branch facts and audit the route estimates. [Tribal Health Expert Committee report](https://nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/Tribal_Report/Tribal_Health_Report.pdf) · [Datathon ranking method](../non-ml/QUALITY_METHOD.md) |
| **Candidate predictors for later testing** | Add dated, branch-specific audits of the features above, observed registration or wait times, service hours, and staffing or workload measures tied to that service. Test whether they predict the survey outcome on held-out data. Treat ownership, accreditation and capacity as contextual variables to test, with special attention to whether they mostly reflect documentation coverage. Keep district demographics, names, review text and proxy-inferred identities out of individual scoring. [WHO facility assessment modules](https://www.who.int/data/data-collection-tools/harmonized-health-facility-assessment/introduction) · [Obermeyer et al., proxy-target bias, *Science*, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31649194/) |

For a first study, choose one clinical endpoint and one primary experience measure. In maternity care, use a locally tested **Person-Centered Maternity Care (PCMC) score**, with privacy, consent and reported mistreatment visible as separate items. Afulani et al. validated a 27-item scale in 2,018 postpartum participants across 40 government facilities in Uttar Pradesh. It covers dignity and respect, communication and autonomy, and supportive care. Other services need their own instrument and validation. In an older-adult analysis of India’s LASI survey, the available experience measures included wait, respect, clarity, privacy, choice of provider and cleanliness. [Afulani et al., *Reproductive Health*, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12978-018-0591-7) · [LASI patient-experience analysis, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11413678/)

Ask concrete questions about what happened. In an Odisha pilot adapting the US Hospital CAHPS patient-experience questionnaire, six of 18 questions were inconsistently understood during testing. In a follow-up analysis of the 507 inpatient interviews from the Odisha assessment, 61.8% of Scheduled Tribe respondents and 32.5% of respondents in the study’s general category reported disrespect. Overall dissatisfaction ranged from 1.3% to 3.6% across the four reported caste categories. In a separate Uttar Pradesh childbirth study, 9.1% of participants reported mistreatment on six matched items, compared with 22.4% documented by observers. Translate and test how people interpret questions, gather feedback privately and compare reports with independent observations where feasible. Review stars and complaint counts lack the sampling and denominators needed to estimate the frequency of mistreatment. [Woskie et al., *Bulletin of the World Health Organization*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.2471/blt.24.290519) · [Dey et al., *Reproductive Health*, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5688759/)

The closest India-specific precedents are survey instruments, facility standards, accessibility audits and focused studies of differential treatment. The targeted literature and data review found **no publicly documented and validated India-wide tool** that predicts service-specific patient experience by demographic group for named branches in this project. Existing tools can inform a pilot. The first data milestone is an authorized, representative-enough set of outcomes that actually matches the question the feature would answer.

## How a prediction could work

For the clinical endpoint, start with observed counts, rates and uncertainty from comparable cohorts. For hospital comparisons, adjust for relevant clinical differences present at entry, such as urgency, referral status, comorbidities and severity. Check whether facilities capture events after transfer or discharge with similar completeness. Report standardization choices and publish raw and adjusted estimates where informative. Examine whether differences persist within self-identified groups, with adequate event counts and comparable case mix. Smaller facilities and rare outcomes may need a longer reporting window or rates pooled across a region. Treat adjusted results as estimates for comparable cohorts, with underlying uncertainty about outcomes for an individual patient. [CAG audit referral example](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2024/7_Chapter-3-Healthcare-Services-067c18f3519e939.35643486.pdf#page=31) · [Dialysis multilevel outcomes study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38601176/) · [Clinical prediction model validation guidance](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-074820)

For respectful care, estimate experience using its own survey cohort and denominator. Present its findings alongside the outcome rate so that high marks in one measure cannot obscure weakness or missing evidence in the other.

1. **Define the population and comparison.** For a given branch and service, estimate the average experience score or the share reporting a concrete favorable experience among patients in a defined period. Report a branch’s all-patient level, the group-specific level and the within-branch group gap against a prespecified reference. Also compare the same group across comparable branches. An illustrative facility where two groups report 55% favorable experience has a zero-point gap, while one reporting 85% and 90% has a five-point gap and better absolute experience for both groups. A study of US hospitals found that overall experience and group gaps can diverge. The US Lown Index provides a precedent for a separate community-inclusivity measure based on whom a hospital serves. It answers an access question that differs from how people experience a visit. [Elliott et al., *Medical Care*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1097/MLR.0000000000001949) · [Lown Institute methodology](https://lownhospitalsindex.org/rankings/our-methodology/)
2. **Start with measurements and simple baselines.** Publish approved branch-service rates with their periods, eligible and responding counts, and intervals. For pilot modeling, compare a pooled service baseline, branch-only estimates, and a hierarchical model that lets branch and selected group effects vary. Use a model suitable for the prespecified outcome, such as hierarchical logistic regression for a yes/no experience item. Partial pooling lets branches share information while preserving uncertainty. Add verified operational features and a few predeclared interactions only if they improve held-out performance. An intersectional multilevel model is a research option once the sample supports it. [Evans et al., intersectional multilevel analysis tutorial, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssmph.2024.101664)
3. **Keep the source of a difference interpretable.** Show raw and standardized results, with a written rationale for each adjustment. For a like-for-like comparison, one analysis could compare patients with a similar service and pre-visit clinical need. Measure barriers such as inability to book, payment problems and wait as outcomes or mechanisms in their own right. Compare estimates by response mode and enrollment channel. Experiences observed among patients who attended a branch remain conditional on attendance. A regression on those visits alone cannot tell what an individual would have experienced elsewhere.
4. **Require evidence before changing the order.** Prespecify a meaningful difference, assess precision and re-identification risk, and check response coverage, overlap and measurement comparability. Test future periods for branches with existing data and hold out entire branches or networks to assess transfer. Check error, calibration and interval coverage overall and by group, service and geography. Compare rank stability and the share of patients receiving a useful option against ordinary search and needs-only comparison. Retain the usual display order when the interval for the difference between options includes a practically unimportant difference or the cohort is unsupported. Show “limited comparable feedback” for those cases. [Collins et al., TRIPOD+AI, *BMJ*, 2024](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078378) · [External validation guidance, *BMJ*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-074820)

## Product design and data governance

Show **Clinical outcomes** and **Respectful care** side by side when the evidence supports both. A clinical result should show the named procedure, event count and denominator, period, follow-up window, source and whether the estimate accounts for differences in patient risk. The respect result should show what respondents were asked, how many responded and when. Use “Comparable data unavailable” where evidence is insufficient. Let users compare access needs and travel along with these measures, with a clear option to continue without providing preferences. Keep clinically appropriate service and location checks ahead of personalization. Use elective or planned-care searches for the pilot and preserve the urgency-first workflow for emergencies. [Datathon evidence and ranking method](../non-ml/QUALITY_METHOD.md)

For the research pilot, co-design optional demographics with Dalit and Adivasi participants, religious minorities, disabled and transgender patients and other relevant local groups. Ask for self-identification in participants’ own terms, with a skip option per question. Collect only fields tied to prespecified questions, potentially caste or community, religion, gender identity, age band, or disability or access needs. Language and scheme preferences can generally be requested directly for search. Avoid identity inference from names, addresses, language or inferred household characteristics. Keep distinct consent for study participation and later personalization. Treat a skipped field as missing and test how missingness affects who the system can serve. [ICMR ethics guidelines for health research](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/ICMR_Ethical_Guidelines_2017.pdf) · [ICMR ethical guidelines for AI in healthcare](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/Ethical-guidelines/Ethical_Guidelines_AI_Healthcare_2023.pdf)

Use session-only choices wherever practical. Keep demographic selections out of URLs, external routing calls, analytics, general logs and hospital referrals. For the separately consented study, restrict access, separate contact information, set a retention period, support withdrawal while records remain identifiable and release only reviewed aggregates. Suppress small or readily identifiable cells, including cases where differences between published views reveal a suppressed count. Confirm safeguards with research partners before recruitment and test whether people understand who can see their answers. [ICMR health research ethics guidelines](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/ICMR_Ethical_Guidelines_2017.pdf)

India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act and 2025 Rules have phased commencement. As of November 21, 2026, specified consent-manager provisions had begun, with core processing and consent provisions scheduled for May 13, 2027. Plan for specific, informed and affirmative consent, data minimization, security, withdrawal and applicable access and erasure rights. The Act’s research exemption is conditional and excludes processing used to take decisions specific to a person. During the transition, assess whether the 2011 sensitive personal data rules apply to the project. If an integration enters the ABDM ecosystem, review its health data policies and consent requirements. Confirm the applicable legal and ethics obligations with the institution or partner before collecting real data. [DPDP Act, §§6, 8, 11–13, 17](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22037/1/a2023-22.pdf) · [Commencement Gazette](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/267647.pdf) · [DPDP Rules Gazette](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/267650.pdf) · [MeitY consent-manager update, November 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2336151&lang=2&reg=48) · [2011 SPDI Rules](https://meity.gov.in/sites/upload_files/dit/files/GSR313E_10511%281%29.pdf) · [MoHFW on ABDM health data and security](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2152537&lang=2&reg=48)

The product can explain rights and direct users to the relevant grievance route. For example, section 25 of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act calls for measures including barrier-free access and priority attendance. Use local rules when presenting enforceable rights, since the Clinical Establishments Act’s adoption varies by jurisdiction. ## Pilot and future research

A randomized phone study with roughly 1,100 Rajasthan patients receiving chronic kidney disease care tested information about insurance entitlements and other participating providers. It improved awareness and lowered payments for patients at public hospitals, with no detectable overall spending reduction across public and private patients. The next question for Datathon is whether better information changes the choice and the experience of care across the patients this search is intended to serve. [Dupas and Jain, *Journal of Public Economics*, 2023](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10156818/)

1. **Demonstrate the feature with verifiable needs.** Select a small set of applicable searches and branches. Record the exact branch, service, stated support, source, verification method and date. Use the existing evidence and location gates. Show the two views side by side with examples of trade-offs and missing evidence. Illustrate future survey cards with clearly marked fictional data. The project already has confirmed service recommendations and a patient-rating import contract that can inform the design. The Streamlit dashboard currently has its own service matching and ranking, so use the shared evidence workflow before enabling any patient-facing reranking. [Datathon project context](CONTEXT_AND_HISTORY.md) · [Datathon ranking and ratings import](../non-ml/QUALITY_METHOD.md)
2. **Pilot both measures in one service and region.** Maternity care is the strongest starting point found in this review because LaQshya already defines facility reporting fields and Uttar Pradesh has a validated experience instrument. Begin with several comparable partner sites. One feasible study cohort is patients undergoing caesarean delivery, with consistent 30-day surgical-site infection follow-up and a private PCMC survey offered to the same cohort. Start by auditing one reporting period against operation and follow-up records. Request a stable branch and service ID, eligible procedure count, outcome and timing, follow-up and transfer status, and baseline clinical risk fields. For the research study, use a partner-held study ID to link consented surveys and outcomes. Publish only approved aggregates. Capture people who delayed, abandoned or were denied care through a separate community access cohort. Size the study on expected clinical event counts as well as survey participation, including by group. For scale, even 250 independent survey responses per group with favorable rates near 80% give an approximate 95% margin of error of ±7 percentage points for the difference, before nonresponse or clustering. [LaQshya Annexure 10](https://nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/RMNCH_MH_Guidelines/LaQshya-Guidance-note-Annexures.pdf) · [PCMC validation](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12978-018-0591-7) · [Odisha patient-experience survey](https://doi.org/10.2471/blt.24.290519)
3. **Test whether optional personalization adds value.** Use scenario and usability testing first, then compare ordinary search, a view with outcomes and respect for all patients, and a view with optional group-level evidence once the estimates qualify. Test whether people understand each measure and find a feasible branch, and monitor completed care, clinical events, costs and reported experience where the study can measure them. Audit added travel, exclusion of nearby alternatives, participation burden, subgroup coverage and risk of disclosure. Any benefit from sharing demographics should be measured against the same finder without that step.
4. **Study transfer, intersectionality and improvement.** Test whether the same questions mean the same thing across languages, survey modes and care settings. Compare patterns across public and private branches, states and intersections chosen with community partners. Study whether auditable changes such as adding interpreters or changing registration procedures improve measured experience. In Uttar Pradesh, a matched evaluation of locally designed maternity quality improvements found a 22.9-point increase on a 100-point experience scale relative to controls. Use community follow-up to reach people who never obtained care and independent audits or ethics-approved standardized patients where appropriate to investigate mechanisms. Evaluate whether publishing information changes referral patterns or encourages facilities to address the barriers.
