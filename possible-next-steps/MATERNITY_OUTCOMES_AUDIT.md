# Maternity Outcomes and Patient Reported Respect at Named Indian Hospitals

**Datathon evidence audit · November 21, 2026**

Public sources document overlaps at named hospitals. **KIMS Healthcare** and **Guru Teg Bahadur Hospital** reported clinical outcomes and patient feedback for the same small delivery cohorts with known dates. **JIPMER** published delivery outcomes and direct reports of disrespect for the exact same two months, with different patient groups. **Lok Nayak Hospital** reported preterm births and a measure of respect in the same sample, with recruitment months unspecified. These historical cohorts show what can be collected together. A hospital ranking would require comparable outcome definitions, patient groups and follow-up.

## Demographic breakdowns in the published studies

**None of the seven named-hospital studies published both a clinical outcome and a patient-reported respect measure broken down by the same demographic groups in the same cohort.** The closest studies collected information that could support a reanalysis, with limits set by sample size, eligibility and access to the underlying data.

| Hospital | What the publication provides |
| --- | --- |
| **Lok Nayak Hospital** | In the 300-patient sample, the authors tested age, caste, religion, education and socioeconomic status against the person-centred maternity care score. They detected no statistically significant associations for those characteristics. The samples for Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe patients were eight and three, respectively. The paper reports 35 preterm births for the full cohort with no breakdown across those same groups. It also reports an association between the baby's sex and patient-reported experience, with no clinical outcome reported by baby sex. The paper says its underlying data may be available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request. [Mandal et al., tables 1, 2 and 6 and data availability](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12884-025-08211-w) |
| **JK Hospital** | Tables 1–2 relate respect scores to age, residence, education and socioeconomic status. From table 2, high respect scores occurred in **27 of 41** upper, **9 of 25** middle and **4 of 14** lower socioeconomic-group participants. Table 3 groups newborn outcomes by the respect score. The published tables therefore give no newborn outcome rates within those socioeconomic groups. The counts are small and unadjusted, and some of the separately reported clinical results have denominator problems. [Singhai et al., tables 1–3](https://www.jptcp.com/index.php/jptcp/article/download/10806/10294/22150#page=5) |
| **JIPMER** | The survey tested age, education, socioeconomic status and other characteristics against reports of disrespect or abuse. It did not detect a statistically significant predictor. Its stillbirth, eclampsia and newborn intensive-care counts came from cases the survey excluded. [Dorairajan et al., methods and factors affecting negative experience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7960873/) |
| **KIMS Healthcare** | The paper gives cohort-level clinical outcomes and childbirth experience. A few experience questions apply to vaginal births only. The published tables give no demographic groups with both measures. [Ahmed et al., tables 1–4](https://www.academia.edu/84580565/Birthing_Experience_and_Perinatal_Outcomes_with_COVID_19_A_Prospective_Study) |
| **Guru Teg Bahadur Hospital** | The questionnaire asked about age, religion, education, occupation and socioeconomic status. Its stillbirth and newborn test results and its count of reported physical or verbal abuse are cohort totals. [Aggarwal et al., table 1, results and questionnaire](https://www.academia.edu/116203429/Childbirth_Experience_of_Mothers_in_COVID_Times) |
| **District Hospital Purnea** | The public figures give monthly counts of clinical events and an overall satisfaction score. They give neither individual respect items nor demographic breakdowns. [Reddy and Ali, figures 1–2](https://www.ijoparb.co.in/assets/journal/small_journal/OA02.pdf#page=3) |
| **MediCiti Institute of Medical Sciences** | The infection study analyses maternal age and other risk factors. Its methods describe collection from clinical records and follow-up for infection symptoms, with no patient-reported respect measure in the paper. The publisher lists a questionnaire and a workbook that could not be opened during this check. [Basany et al., table 3 and supporting information](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286165) |

### Access to the Lok Nayak study data

The journal releases cohort tables for the 300 participants, including demographics and obstetric history on PDF page 6. Its data availability statement restricts open release for confidentiality and directs reasonable requests to corresponding author Irena Mandal. The Research Square preprint gives the same access route. Searches by title, DOI and registration number yielded no public participant dataset or data supplement. The individual CTRI record, **CTRI/2023/07/055918**, could not be retrieved through the registry lookup. [Published tables, p. 6](https://bmcpregnancychildbirth.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12884-025-08211-w.pdf#page=6) · [Journal data availability](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12884-025-08211-w#data-availability) · [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-6896944/v1) · [CTRI lookup](https://ctri.nic.in/Clinicaltrials/searchbyctri.php)

### A pooled study with both measures by caste and wealth

Raj et al. surveyed **2,639 patients across 68 Uttar Pradesh public facilities** in 2015. Tables 2–3 provide counts to calculate the following shares within the same demographic groups. Both mistreatment and delivery complications were reported by patients. The results are pooled across facilities, and the two percentages for each group do not show how many people reported both. [Raj et al., 2017, study design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28676965/) · [Full text, tables 2–3](https://www.academia.edu/117630850/Associations_Between_Mistreatment_by_a_Provider_during_Childbirth_and_Maternal_Health_Complications_in_Uttar_Pradesh_India)

| Group as recorded in the study | Reported mistreatment during childbirth | Reported delivery complications |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe, combined | 180/800 (22.5%) | 350/800 (43.8%) |
| Other Backward Classes | 328/1,546 (21.2%) | 686/1,546 (44.4%) |
| Other caste group | 44/293 (15.0%) | 173/293 (59.0%) |
| Lowest wealth-index quintile | 125/528 (23.7%) | 246/528 (46.6%) |
| Highest wealth-index quintile | 98/528 (18.6%) | 209/528 (39.6%) |

The differences are descriptive and unadjusted. The paper tabulates religion for the mistreatment comparison, with no corresponding complication-by-religion breakdown in tables 3–4. This is a regional example of a same-cohort demographic analysis. The Datathon use case also needs named-branch results and comparable ascertainment of clinical events. [Raj et al., full text, tables 2–4](https://www.academia.edu/117630850/Associations_Between_Mistreatment_by_a_Provider_during_Childbirth_and_Maternal_Health_Complications_in_Uttar_Pradesh_India)

## Interpretation for hospital search

KIMS and Guru Teg Bahadur are the clearest dated examples of clinical records and feedback from the same patients. Their respective communication and abuse questions cover different aspects of respectful care. JIPMER shows why matching by facility and date is only part of the job. A survey can omit the very patients whose outcomes the hospital tracks. Lok Nayak's preterm count comes from the same interview sample, and gestational age at delivery also reflects factors before patients reached the hospital. The public results are study aggregates with insufficient coverage for comparing the same subgroup across hospitals. [KIMS study](https://www.jsafog.com/abstractArticleContentBrowse/JSAFOG/6/14/3/29649/abstractArticle/Article) · [Guru Teg Bahadur study](https://www.academia.edu/116203429/Childbirth_Experience_of_Mothers_in_COVID_Times) · [JIPMER study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7960873/) · [Lok Nayak study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12884-025-08211-w)

For a Datathon demonstration, KIMS and JIPMER can illustrate how to show two evidence measures with their own sample descriptions and dates. For a live comparison, request a common clinical outcome and the same respect questions from a shared eligible cohort of patients at each partner branch. Capture responses and follow-up separately so a missing survey response or unknown clinical outcome remains visible. [Datathon research report](HEALTH_EQUITY_REPORT.md)
