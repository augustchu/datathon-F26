# Equity in Hospital Recommendations

**Datathon research and feature report · November 21, 2026**

A useful hospital recommendation considers whether patients can access and afford a service, how they fare clinically, and whether they report being treated with respect. The answers can differ among people at the same hospital. The optional equity score combines patient-reported respect and clinical outcomes when comparable data are available. **The public maternity evidence reviewed here cannot support a live score or current hospital comparisons by caste, religion, income or another demographic.** It provides a starting point for collecting the necessary data. [India health equity review](HEALTH_EQUITY_REPORT.md) · [Named-hospital maternity audit](MATERNITY_OUTCOMES_AUDIT.md)

## Why this belongs in search

- **An average can conceal differences in care.** In 1,485 Odisha patient interviews, people with more education or higher caste reported better interpersonal treatment within the same facility. Scheduled Tribe respondents were 14 percentage points less likely to report dignity and respect across the study. A separate Odisha hospital survey also found that a disparity in reported disrespect could be missed by an overall satisfaction question. These studies motivate direct questions about what happened during the visit. [Yip et al., 2022](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23288604.2022.2132366) · [Woskie et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197647/)
- **Financial coverage and actual costs need separate measures.** Rajasthan insurance research found that eligible patients still faced costs and that higher costs were associated with larger gender gaps in utilization. A searchable scheme listing can help patients identify a place to check. An evaluation still needs to record patient eligibility, package authorization, out-of-pocket spending and care that was delayed or forgone. [Dupas and Jain, 2024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10198787/)
- **Respect and clinical outcomes answer distinct questions.** A patient can recover and still experience abuse or care without consent. Clinical records capture events such as an infection once a person receives care. Private patient interviews can capture dignity, privacy, understandable communication and autonomy. In an Uttar Pradesh study, 9.1% of women reported mistreatment on six comparable items while observers recorded 22.4%. Survey timing, privacy and wording therefore matter, and direct observation can help audit the measurement. [Afulani et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6114501/) · [Dey et al., 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12978-017-0409-z)

## What the equity score does in recommendations

**Consider equity score** is a single toggle, off by default. The **What's that?** button opens this explanation. The search compares branch-level evidence, so everyone sees the same paired measures. [Dashboard implementation](../dashboard/dashboard.py)

> Women and people from marginalized groups can receive worse care. The equity score combines patient-reported respect and clinical outcomes to help choose between similar hospitals.

For a branch with a qualifying record, the prototype averages the percentage of patients who report respectful care and the risk-adjusted percentage who have a favorable clinical outcome. Both measures must refer to the same eligible cohort. A comparable pair must share the service, dates, cohort definition, clinical outcome and follow-up, respect measure, and risk adjustment method. Each component must have at least 30 observations and documented uncertainty bounds. Thirty observations are a prototype floor. A study may need substantially more to achieve useful precision, particularly for rare outcomes and subgroup estimates. [Paired evidence contract](../non-ml/src/facility_tool/equity.py)

A branch can move up only when its score’s lower bound exceeds the other branch’s upper bound and the options are otherwise similar. Each move keeps the same service or capability tier and allows at most 1 km of additional straight-line distance. Where routes are comparable, it also allows at most 1 km of extra route distance and 2 additional minutes of travel. The order stays the same when evidence is unavailable or inconclusive, during time-critical searches, and in the notebook’s closest sorting mode. [Existing Datathon ranking method](../non-ml/QUALITY_METHOD.md) · [Paired equity ranking rules](../non-ml/src/facility_tool/equity.py)

**Current coverage.** The reviewed live evidence file has no qualifying branch records. The prototype reports that a comparison is unavailable in live searches and uses clearly labeled fictional records in demo mode. The earlier access audit remains a source for researching financial barriers, including JSSK cashless maternity entitlements in Delhi and hemodialysis scheme listings in Tamil Nadu and Assam. The equity score requires measured patient experiences and clinical results. ## Data required for a clinically useful equity comparison

| Input | What to collect and why |
| --- | --- |
| **Branch, service and eligible cohort** | Stable branch and service IDs, exact dates, care setting, eligibility, planned or emergency status, and referral and transfer status. Compare a common service and window. Record everyone eligible and show who was missed by follow-up and surveys. |
| **Clinical outcome and baseline risk** | Start with 30-day surgical-site infection after caesarean delivery. Use a common event definition, clinical verification, event date and denominator. Track transfers and outcomes after discharge. Candidate risk variables include elective versus emergency procedure, comorbidities and referral status measured at presentation. Decide adjustment variables in advance and check whether adjustments mask inequities from delayed access or referral. Alongside this post-caesarean measure, record caesarean indication and receipt of needed care across the wider delivery cohort to see who enters the surgical denominator. [MediCiti prospective surveillance protocol](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286165) · [LaQshya facility reporting fields, annexure 10](https://nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/RMNCH_MH_Guidelines/LaQshya-Guidance-note-Annexures.pdf#page=19) |
| **Patient-reported respect** | Use consistent, translated questions about dignity, consent, privacy, communication and support, based on the Person-Centered Maternity Care scale. Offer a confidential survey independent of the care team. Record the people invited, the people who answered, languages, survey mode, timing, item scores and missing answers. [Afulani et al., India validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6114501/) |
| **Self-described demographics and barriers** | In the separately consented study, co-design optional group questions tied to specific hypotheses. Candidates include caste or community, religion, an affordability or income band, disability, language and age. Ask directly about interpreters, accessible treatment areas, coverage, informal costs and referrals. Link surveys and clinical records through a study ID held by the partner. Keep consent and sensitive research data separate from ordinary search, and publish disclosure-reviewed aggregates. [ICMR research ethics guidelines](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/ICMR_Ethical_Guidelines_2017.pdf) |

**Coverage and precision decide when a comparison is usable.** Plan sample sizes from the number of clinical events, survey responses and protected cells required at each branch. For scale, applying MediCiti’s historical 4.6% infection rate to 1,000 procedures would yield about 46 events before any demographic split. Report event counts, eligible denominators, follow-up, survey response and uncertainty. Show overall and subgroup estimates and compare each subgroup’s absolute results across similar branches. Publish a subgroup difference only when the estimates meet prespecified precision and disclosure thresholds. Record unavailable estimates explicitly. [MediCiti incidence results](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286165#sec003) · [ICMR research ethics guidelines](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/ICMR_Ethical_Guidelines_2017.pdf)

**Compare outcomes and respect side by side.** A small disparity between groups can coexist with poor outcomes for everyone. Once data qualify, show the absolute, risk-standardized clinical outcome and the respect measure for each comparable branch. Report within-branch gaps separately. If better outcomes at one branch accompany worse patient-reported respect, show both so people can weigh the trade-off. Test the prototype’s equal weighting with patient partners before using it for live comparisons. [WHO standards for maternal and newborn care](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241511216) · [India PCMC validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6114501/)

## Research priorities

1. **Start with comparable maternity branches.** Use LaQshya registers to find candidate sites and reconcile one shared reporting period with operation registers, transfer records and post-discharge follow-up. Ask the Lok Nayak authors for dates and feasible aggregate analyses as a feasibility step. Recruit patients who experienced complications through a protocol developed with patient partners. Include community recruitment for people who could not obtain care. [LaQshya monthly facility reporting template](https://nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/RMNCH_MH_Guidelines/LaQshya-Guidance-note-Annexures.pdf#page=19) · [Lok Nayak eligibility and data availability](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12884-025-08211-w)
2. **Test measurement and transfer.** Check whether translations and survey modes measure the same concepts. Compare response and follow-up across groups. Test a risk-standardized clinical model and a separate respect model on later periods and held-out sites. Check calibration and uncertainty by group. Use an appropriate multilevel model only after establishing coverage, and examine whether estimates change under plausible assumptions about missing outcomes. Keep actual rates and uncertainty visible beside any future rank.
3. **Test whether the ranking helps patients reach care.** Compare standard search with the optional ranking. Measure completed care, delays, added travel, payments, reported respect and prespecified clinical outcomes where the pilot can follow them. Monitor how often paired data are missing and whether patients understand why results moved. Test whether group-specific estimates add value only when the data, consent and user testing support that comparison. [Rajasthan insurance information experiment](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10156818/) · [Full Datathon research plan](HEALTH_EQUITY_REPORT.md)
