# Find the right care

Living project summary for the CMU Datathon, Fall 2026. Updated 21 November 2026. Update the evidence snapshot, decisions, and evaluation results below as the team runs new work.

## The challenge

A health worker, NGO coordinator or district planner enters a service and a location. The tool returns a short list of nearby facility branches with the exact service wording, its source and scope, the basis for the location and ranking, and a published phone number where available. A reviewer should be able to reproduce why the first result was chosen and see why plausible alternatives were excluded. The submission must use the challenge's Virtue Foundation facility data, run on Databricks Free Edition, and demonstrate a clear workflow for a nontechnical user.

**Current state.** The repo contains modular Python notebooks, offline snapshots, a fictional walkthrough, and blocked-access records. The public-snapshot audit ran locally. The original FDR table was unavailable locally, so the FDR EDA and an end-to-end run on Free Edition remain outstanding. The new regression and ML modes accept existing estimates. The fictional walkthrough uses handwritten estimates and runs on CPU. An earlier H100 probe in a trial workspace returned an explicit AI Runtime trial restriction. Workspace entitlements and the Free Edition run still need direct verification when authorized. [Databricks Free Edition limitations](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/getting-started/free-edition-limitations)

## Decisions and iterations

1. **Preserve source rows and link branches conservatively.** Notebook 02 keeps a location audit for the original address, city, district, state, PIN and coordinates, alongside normalized values, geography flags and branch-level review. Use `location_records` source by source, `location_entity_review` at branch level, and `location_quality_summary` for counts of flagged issues. A unique same-name match also needs supporting address or coordinate evidence. Retain ambiguous links and location disagreements for inspection. The Kerala SHA Ascent row pairs a Malappuram district label with a Kozhikode address. An area point can resolve a query to a place when clearly labeled. Ranking a branch requires usable branch coordinates. A published branch state that conflicts with every state for its PIN requires review before distance ranking.
2. **Require a citable service statement for recommendations.** Notebook 04 records the source excerpt, field, URL, dates, scheme or directory scope, and stance. An applicable contradictory claim can block a recommendation. Weak matches, expired or suspended scope, and uncertain linkage remain visible for review. Distinguish service subtypes such as NICU Level III and newborn stabilization units, and blood banks and storage units. Scheme dates give evidence validity. Current staffing, openings and admission remain questions for the facility.
3. **Use distance and route time with different labels.** The query accepts a recognized service and coordinates, a city, district or PIN. Resolve ambiguous place names with a state or more precise location. The ranker orders eligible branches by evidence validity tier and then proximity. Straight-line distance is the default. Explicitly enabling TravelTime for real inputs can supply estimated travel minutes. If the comparison lacks complete route coverage, distance supplies a common ranking basis. Missing routes and approximate area origins stay visible. [TravelTime route matrix documentation](https://docs.traveltime.com/api/reference/travel-time-distance-matrix)
4. **Show one query in three modes.** Confirmed uses documented evidence and source-reported fields. ML-assisted adds tagged estimates for missing fields from a reviewed model artifact. Regression-assisted adds tagged estimates from an interpretable statistical model. The modes share the service evidence gate. Model-only service scores generate follow-up leads with their own label. The comparison displays value provenance and intervals where supplied. The fit score is a point-based summary of direct evidence, validity, origin precision and proximity. It is separate from the ranking trace and from any estimate of availability.
5. **Call, adapt, then review.** Notebook 07 shows the original recommendation and any citable facility number. A caller's No removes the branch for that service and this search across all three modes and replenishes the shortlist when supported alternatives exist. A Yes annotates this search. After alternatives are shown, a separate explicit action can send an unresolved listing to the SQLite moderation stack. New reports appear newest first, and moderators can distinguish no answer, temporary unavailability, a service the branch says it does not offer, and wrong details. Decision history is append-only. Source changes and training labels require a separate review. The notebook exercises these operations without a frontend or a deployed moderation service.
6. **Opt in to spreading demand.** Keep the normal ranking as the default. The selector bypasses reranking for emergency or trauma service categories. Notebook 08 compares nearby options with comparable service evidence within a configurable extra-travel limit. When service-specific demand and capacity share a unit, period and basis, the selector can combine a normalized detour penalty with demand pressure. Fresh compatible measured availability can also inform selection. Existing bed totals and doctor counts describe resources. Slot availability for dialysis or another service requires its own measurement. Real load inputs remain absent from the current snapshots, and scenario outputs describe hypothetical recommendations shown. Evidence from a health system with known appointments illustrates how travel constraints and service capacity can inform redistribution. [Moore and Huang, chemotherapy appointment reallocation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12107644/)

## Analysis before fitting

The project began with a random forest for the missing capacity column. The earlier notebook and GPU experiments are under [`ML/`](../ML/). The new work in [`non-ml/notebooks/`](notebooks/) starts with source coverage and a separate facility-level count audit, then proceeds to service evidence, provenance, optional district context, mode comparison, and load scenarios. Checkpoints let the team rerun each step with revised inputs or rules. The new count audit covers capacity and doctor counts, checks raw ranges and units, excludes conflicting labels, and compares coverage by geography, source and facility type. Each candidate predictor must occur strictly after `countries` in the original FDR schema. Leakage checks flag repeated target text. The notebook uses a structured subset for relationships and interactions, including facility type, operator, and established year when eligible.

Use the EDA to choose a count baseline and compare it with interpretable regression and any retained random forest on the same facility-held test set. A median is a useful baseline for MAE. Evaluate several reasonable count specifications if dispersion or concentrated zeros warrant it. Report held-out MAE and tail errors, interval coverage and width, test size, and performance across important subgroups. Test geographic transfer on geographically separated groups where feasible. Observed-count holdouts measure reconstruction in the observed cohort. Missing-count performance needs independently recovered counts or a carefully designed validation exercise. Report the impact on recommendations separately from count error. [Alomair, count models and zeros](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11687910/) · [Linnenbrink et al., spatial validation](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/5897/2024/gmd-17-5897-2024.html) · [Nikitina and Mouriño, imputation accuracy and downstream performance](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-026-01295-3)

A logistic regression is appropriate for a binary service target if independently reviewed positives and negatives exist. Source silence, unanswered calls and model leads stay unlabeled. Assess precision, coverage, calibration and false-positive examples near the chosen threshold. Report abstentions separately. A clinical-text study provides a precedent for comparing interpretable logistic regression with more complex text models. Reporting guidance offers a template for documenting labels, splits and performance. [Sumsion et al., interpretable text classification comparison](https://medinform.jmir.org/2025/1/e64113/) · [TRIPOD+AI reporting guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078378)

## What a strong submission shows

- Run a real challenge-data search on **Databricks Free Edition**. Record the data snapshot and the notebook configuration. Show a workflow a health worker can follow with a service and a place as the only required inputs.
- Walk through one strong recommendation, one excluded or ambiguous branch, and a search with no reliable match. Show the excerpt, source link, scope, validity, travel measure and ranking logic directly next to each result.
- Hold the query fixed when switching the three modes. Point to each field that changed, its evidence or model provenance, and what the team measured on held-out data. Use the fictional walkthrough as a labeled test when real estimates are unavailable.
- Demonstrate the ordinary ranking and the opt-in load setting on the same query. State the detour cap and load units, flag whether the load input is observed or hypothetical, and show the fallback when the inputs are absent or incomparable. Make clear whether the comparison reflects changed recommendations or measured changes in crowding.
- Show a published contact and a caller's No leading to an alternative or a clear no-alternative result. Keep the report, moderation decision and source evidence traceable. Document coverage gaps, blocked sources, the paused statistical audit and any unrun evaluation.
