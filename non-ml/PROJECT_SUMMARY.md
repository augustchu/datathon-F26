# Find the right care

Living project summary for the CMU Datathon, Fall 2026. Updated 21 November 2026. Update the evidence snapshot, decisions, and evaluation results below as the team runs new work.

## The challenge

A health worker, NGO coordinator or district planner enters a service and a location. The tool returns a short list of nearby facility branches with the exact service wording, its source and scope, the basis for the location and ranking, and a published phone number where available. A reviewer should be able to reproduce why the first result was chosen and see why plausible alternatives were excluded. The submission must use the challenge's Virtue Foundation facility data, run on Databricks Free Edition, and demonstrate a clear workflow for a nontechnical user.

**Current state.** The repo contains modular Python notebooks, saved public snapshots and a fictional walkthrough. The original facilities, India Post and NFHS tables were read from the team’s Free Edition workspace. The EDA, lookup and CPU count models ran locally on private copies and saved public data. An end-to-end run of the notebooks inside Free Edition remains outstanding. A historical H100 probe on a trial workspace returned an AI Runtime trial restriction. [Databricks Free Edition limitations](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/getting-started/free-edition-limitations)

## Decisions and iterations

1. **Preserve source rows and link branches conservatively.** Notebook 02 keeps a location audit for the original address, city, district, state, PIN and coordinates, alongside normalized values, geography flags and branch-level review. Use `location_records` source by source, `location_entity_review` at branch level, and `location_quality_summary` for counts of flagged issues. A unique same-name match also needs supporting address or coordinate evidence. Retain ambiguous links and location disagreements for inspection. The Kerala SHA Ascent row pairs a Malappuram district label with a Kozhikode address. A published branch state that conflicts with every state for its PIN requires review before distance ranking. Each facility saves its eligibility flag and hold reason during preparation.
2. **Require a citable service statement for recommendations.** Notebook 04 records the source excerpt, field, URL, dates, scheme or directory scope, and stance. An applicable contradictory claim can block a recommendation. Weak matches, expired or suspended scope, and uncertain linkage remain visible for review. Distinguish service subtypes such as NICU Level III and newborn stabilization units, and blood banks and storage units. Scheme dates give evidence validity. Current staffing, openings and admission remain questions for the facility.
3. **Use distance and route time with different labels.** The query accepts a recognized service and coordinates, a city, district or PIN. A state-qualified name with conflicting city districts can use a unique matching postal district center, labeled as approximate. The ranker orders candidates by evidence validity tier and then, by default, by distance along the estimated TravelTime driving route. A query can select walking or cycling and sort by distance or time, or choose public transport and sort by time. If comparable route coverage is incomplete, straight-line distance supplies a common ranking basis. Missing routes and approximate origins stay visible. [TravelTime route matrix documentation](https://docs.traveltime.com/api/reference/travel-time-distance-matrix)
4. **Show one query in three modes.** Confirmed uses documented evidence and source-reported fields. ML-assisted adds tagged estimates for missing fields from a reviewed model artifact. Regression-assisted adds tagged estimates from an interpretable statistical model. The modes share the service evidence gate. Model-only service scores generate follow-up leads with their own label. The comparison displays value provenance and intervals where supplied. The fit score is a point-based summary of direct evidence, validity, origin precision and proximity. It is separate from the ranking trace and from any estimate of availability.
5. **Call, adapt, then review.** Notebook 07 shows the original recommendation and any citable facility number. A caller's No removes the branch for that service and this search across all three modes and replenishes the shortlist when supported alternatives exist. A Yes annotates this search. After alternatives are shown, a separate explicit action can send an unresolved listing to the SQLite moderation stack. New reports appear newest first, and moderators can distinguish no answer, temporary unavailability, a service the branch says it does not offer, and wrong details. Decision history is append-only. Source changes and training labels require a separate review. The notebook exercises these operations without a frontend or a deployed moderation service.
6. **Opt in to spreading demand.** Keep the normal ranking as the default. The selector bypasses reranking for emergency or trauma service categories. Notebook 08 compares nearby options with comparable service evidence within a configurable extra-travel limit. When service-specific demand and capacity share a unit, period and basis, the selector can combine a normalized detour penalty with demand pressure. Fresh compatible measured availability can also inform selection. Existing bed totals and doctor counts describe resources. Slot availability for dialysis or another service requires its own measurement. Real load inputs remain absent from the current snapshots, and scenario outputs describe hypothetical recommendations shown. Evidence from a health system with known appointments illustrates how travel constraints and service capacity can inform redistribution. [Moore and Huang, chemotherapy appointment reallocation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12107644/)
7. **Prepare the repeatable work once per snapshot.** Notebook 06b saves indexed place centers, facility routing decisions, valid service claims and excerpts, mode-specific estimates, and the preferred published phone. It groups the candidates and available load inputs by service. The search resolves the request against these stored tables and computes distance, requested route times, ranking preferences and feedback.
8. **Corroborate services with independent publishers.** Notebook 04b distinguishes a direct single-source claim from independent documentary corroboration. It keeps expired, conflicting and insufficient evidence for review. The pilot used seven manually checked statements for three exact branch-service pairs, including dialysis at Soni Hospital in Jaipur and Delhi Heart & Lungs Institute, and cardiac surgery at Star Hospitals Banjara Hills. Reviewers matched the published addresses or PINs before attaching a claim. The combined audit classified 6,617 pairs as single-publisher, 4,563 as insufficient, 10 as conflicting and three as corroborated. Current appointment or treatment availability still needs confirmation. [Reviewed evidence registry](config/reviewed_service_evidence.json)
9. **Verify facility listings before deployment.** The Hindi keypad prototype asks staff to confirm the branch, then answer one question per service the team plans to publish. An optional recording captures services missing from the checklist. Unanswered questions stay unknown. Facility self-reports can support review alongside documentary evidence before the team prepares a release snapshot. The prototype was tested locally and live calling still needs a voice provider connection. [IVR preview](preview_hindi_ivr.py)

## Analysis before fitting

The project began with a random forest for the missing capacity column. The earlier notebook and GPU experiments are under [`ML/`](../ML/). The new work in [`non-ml/notebooks/`](notebooks/) starts with source coverage and a separate facility-level count audit, then proceeds to service evidence, provenance, optional district context, mode comparison, and load scenarios. Checkpoints let the team rerun each step with revised inputs or rules. The new count audit covers capacity and doctor counts, checks raw ranges and units, excludes conflicting labels, and compares coverage by geography, source and facility type. Each candidate predictor must occur strictly after `countries` in the original FDR schema. Leakage checks flag repeated target text. The notebook uses a structured subset for relationships and interactions, including facility type, operator, and established year when eligible.

Use the EDA to choose a count baseline and compare it with interpretable regression and any retained random forest on the same facility-held test set. A median is a useful baseline for MAE. Evaluate several reasonable count specifications if dispersion or concentrated zeros warrant it. Report held-out MAE and tail errors, interval coverage and width, test size, and performance across important subgroups. Test geographic transfer on geographically separated groups where feasible. Observed-count holdouts measure reconstruction in the observed cohort. Missing-count performance needs independently recovered counts or a carefully designed validation exercise. Report the impact on recommendations separately from count error. [Alomair, count models and zeros](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11687910/) · [Linnenbrink et al., spatial validation](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/5897/2024/gmd-17-5897-2024.html) · [Nikitina and Mouriño, imputation accuracy and downstream performance](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-026-01295-3)

### Real count model comparison

The EDA found an unusually long tail. The capacity training median was 100 beds and its maximum was 200,000. The doctor training median was two and its maximum was 6,000. Retain those exact reported labels for the main error report, inspect the large labels separately, and keep their values visible in the source audit. Both fitted approaches used log counts and the same selected predictor blocks. The rule-based view remains available without training.

The model notebook screens for relevant structured fields after `countries`, including facility type, operator type, and the other reported count. Some category fields contain URLs or misplaced numeric data. Phone and phone-derived fields never enter the feature screen. Forward selection uses five folds from the training facilities. A block must improve mean MAE by at least 1% and win at least four folds for both approaches. Only the other reported count and its missingness indicator cleared that gate for each target. The categorical blocks did not. The other count was used only when originally reported, with no cascading estimates.

| Held-out reported counts | Test size | Training median MAE | Log-ridge MAE | Random forest MAE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Beds | 522 | 150.57 | 141.94 | 141.41 |
| Doctors | 745 | 28.82 | 27.83 | 27.63 |

On observed labels, both fitted models improved on the median and remained close to each other. The 90% nominal bed intervals had median width of 653 beds for both fitted approaches. Tail MAE was over 800 beds in both approaches on the 54 test rows with at least 500 beds. These estimates are most appropriate as broad resource context. The release checks restricted overlays to hospital groups with an observed companion count. That supported 922 missing bed counts and 1,126 missing doctor counts per approach. The estimates do not set service eligibility or stand in for staffed slots.

An exploratory wider model had already been scored on this internal holdout before the feature selection rule was refined. The final feature decisions used training folds and the intervals used a separate calibration set. Treat these metrics as an internal comparison. Both geographic transfer and performance on independently recovered missing labels remain unmeasured. [Count model comparison notebook](notebooks/09_count_model_comparison.ipynb)

A logistic regression is appropriate for a binary service target if independently reviewed positives and negatives exist. This snapshot lacks that label set, so the service rules handle the real lookup. Source silence, unanswered calls and model leads stay unlabeled. Assess precision, coverage, calibration and false-positive examples near the chosen threshold. Report abstentions separately. A clinical-text study provides a precedent for comparing interpretable logistic regression with more complex text models. Reporting guidance offers a template for documenting labels, splits and performance. [Sumsion et al., interpretable text classification comparison](https://medinform.jmir.org/2025/1/e64113/) · [TRIPOD+AI reporting guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078378)

## What a strong submission shows

- Run a real challenge-data search on **Databricks Free Edition**. Record the data snapshot and the notebook configuration. Show a workflow a health worker can follow with a service and a place as the only required inputs.
- Walk through one strong recommendation, one excluded or ambiguous branch, and a search with no reliable match. Show the excerpt, source link, scope, validity, travel measure and ranking logic directly next to each result.
- Hold the query fixed when switching the three modes. Point to each field that changed, its evidence or model provenance, and what the team measured on held-out data. Use the fictional walkthrough as a labeled test when real estimates are unavailable.
- Demonstrate the ordinary ranking and the opt-in load setting on the same query. State the detour cap and load units, flag whether the load input is observed or hypothetical, and show the fallback when the inputs are absent or incomparable. Make clear whether the comparison reflects changed recommendations or measured changes in crowding.
- Show a published contact and a caller's No leading to an alternative or a clear no-alternative result. Keep the report, moderation decision and source evidence traceable. Document coverage gaps, blocked sources, the paused statistical audit and any unrun evaluation.
