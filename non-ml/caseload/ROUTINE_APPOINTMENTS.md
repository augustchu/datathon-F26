# Soonest appointment for routine care

**Finding as of November 22, 2026.** Soonest appointment would be a valuable option for routine care. It would let someone weigh a longer journey against receiving the right care sooner. It is **currently impossible to offer or validate with the data available to this project**. We can identify facilities that offer a service and compare travel to them, but we have no provider-confirmed appointment times to rank. The dialysis audit recovered **zero usable slot observations and zero cases** for a real comparison of soonest appointment, nearest eligible, and the current load-balancing policy.

## Why this applies across routine care

The project release has no appointment availability fields. The caseload audit covers historical reports and estimates for 26 facilities. Its MRI scans, dialysis sessions, and other activity counts describe completed or estimated workload over a period. Opening hours describe when a facility operates. Occupancy and demand-to-capacity comparisons describe load when comparable inputs exist. None identifies the next appointment that the provider can offer for the exact service. A nephrology consultation, for example, does not establish a haemodialysis treatment time. The dialysis search was the concrete test case. Broader routine-care scheduling would require coverage for each service in scope. [Caseload audit](EXPANSION.md) · [Current ranking and load inputs](../README.md#travel-settings-and-load-balancing)

With these inputs, nearest eligible can be calculated where service and travel evidence are available. The current load-balancing policy can use comparable availability or pressure where those data exist. We cannot measure how either policy compares with the soonest appointment when the appointment time at each candidate is unknown. The public audit also cannot distinguish a fully booked facility from one whose schedule is inaccessible, so an absent record cannot be counted as zero available slots.

## Data that would make the comparison possible

A provider-authorised, facility-only feed or export would need the exact branch ID, appointment type and eligibility requirements, start time and time zone, observation time, and whether the slot can be booked or only requested. The same decision-time snapshot needs to cover multiple eligible facilities in the same area, with enough information to distinguish missing coverage from a full calendar. Compare all three policies with the same origin, travel estimates, and eligible facilities. Actual booking or completion outcomes would be needed to measure how much sooner patients received care.
