# Typical monthly caseload for Care needed in India

The [network caseload and load-balancing report](NETWORK_CASELOAD.md) documents the all-facility scenario export, the choice of model, and the throughput and replay methods.

The [facility caseload expansion](EXPANSION.md) joins recent published counts to exact facility IDs and tests same-site baselines for additional missing periods across the Care needed options.

The [XGBoost caseload experiment](XGBOOST.md) tests held-out facilities and documents the FY2025–26 dialysis estimates for missing site totals.

The [baseline coverage audit](BASELINES.md) applies the lower-error prior-year average to the linked project facilities and counts eligible fills across all 18 Care needed options.

The baseline estimates typical **reported activity in a calendar month** for each of the 18 options in the dashboard’s service selector. The November 2026 snapshot contains **46 benchmarks from 28 facilities, facility units, or branches across 16 states and union territories**. Each estimate preserves the named site, activity definition, units, reporting period, and source. The sample consists of facilities with usable public reports and supports comparison of reporting sites with similar services.

The starting estimates use counts of visits, admissions, deliveries, scans, operations, and treatment sessions. A person can contribute multiple activities, such as repeat outpatient visits or a series of dialysis sessions. Inpatient admissions count entry to the unit, while bed-days measure time spent in it. These quantities answer different load-planning questions. Reports with a narrow clinical cohort or department scope carry that restriction into the result.

## Calculation

1. **Close the reporting window.** The cutoff is a calendar month. Exclude observations in that month and any later month. The November 2026 cutoff admits counts through October 2026. Exclude a period total if its last month has yet to close.
2. **Use recent observations of the same activity.** Find the latest month with a numeric count in each series. Keep observations from the 12-calendar-month window ending there. Three or more numeric months produce a median and an observed minimum and maximum. Missing values stay missing and published zeros stay zero. The anchor can be old when no later report is available. Read the dates and age with the estimate.
3. **Convert a reported total using its actual period.** Divide the total by the number of covered calendar months. A July-year-to-date total covering April through July would divide by four. A documented study duration can replace inclusive month arithmetic when exact dates are unavailable. Saifai reports two years and labels its dates Dec 2022–Dec 2024, so 2,182 ÷ 24 gives 90.92. [Saifai study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13271864/)
4. **Keep publisher means as labeled.** The NHSRC evaluation supplies monthly averages for the Mirzapur and Chandauli emergency C-section series. It describes one year while its month labels cover 14 calendar months. Preserve the published means and label dates, with an unknown denominator and no monthly range. [NHSRC evaluation, pp. 40 and 48](https://www.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2025-07/PPP%20MCH%20Wing%20in%20Uttar%20Pradesh-Report%20.pdf)
5. **Leave sparse estimates blank.** With fewer than three usable months and no closed-period figure, the result's typical month is null. Reported monthly extrema describe the observed months and give no probabilistic coverage guarantee. A period average gives no month-to-month range.

For Surat MRI, the most recent window available at the cutoff is Oct 2025–Sep 2026. Its 12 counts have a median of **547**, with 474 and 811 as their observed minimum and maximum. The previous nine months are available for earlier testing. For Himalayan Hospital pediatrics, six of eight calendar months between the first and last captured reports have valid counts. September 2025 and February 2026 do not enter the median. For Aravind Coimbatore, **63,598 ÷ 12 = 5,299.83** average operations per month from an annual total. [SMIMER statistics](https://www.suratmunicipal.gov.in/smimer/Hospital/SHOverallStatistics) · [Himalayan clinical material](https://hims.srhu.edu.in/mci-information/) · [Aravind activity report](https://aravind.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Activity-Report-2025-2026.pdf)

## Backtesting

The rolling test estimates each eligible historical month from counts dated before it. It tests the median of a trailing 12-calendar-month window with at least six prior observations. The comparator uses the immediately preceding month's count. Score both on identical months, using mean absolute error (MAE) in each series' activity unit. Require an observed immediately preceding month to score an origin. The model uses event dates for ordering. Publication lags and later revisions are unavailable in most source tables, so this is a retrospective evaluation under the assumption that prior-period counts were available.

The snapshot scores **154 series-months across 20 series**. The rolling median has lower MAE in 7 series and the previous month has lower MAE in 13. This supports keeping the median as a description of typical activity and retaining the previous-month comparator for evaluating short-horizon forecasts. The count-based errors have different units and scales and should be read per series.

| Site and measure | Held-out months | Median MAE | Previous-month MAE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| SMIMER MRI examinations | 15 | 118.17 scans | 70.13 scans |
| SMIMER casualty attendance | 15 | 379.40 visits | 530.67 visits |
| AIIMS Bilaspur NICU total cases | 6 | 5.33 cases | 11.83 cases |
| AIIMS Bilaspur emergency-category stroke cases | 6 | 8.42 cases | 5.67 cases |
| I.T.S Dental College dental OPD | 4 | 2,261.75 registrations | 3,547.50 registrations |

There is no monthly backtest for an annual total, a publisher mean or a series with too few comparable months. The rolling results evaluate the same sites over time. They do not evaluate predictions for facilities outside the sample. For a chronological forecast using a reconstructed snapshot, collect release dates and data vintages with each observation.

## Reproduce or update the snapshot

Run from the repository root with Python 3.10 or newer.

```sh
python3 non-ml/caseload/build.py --output non-ml/caseload/typical_caseload.json
python3 non-ml/caseload/build.py --as-of 2026-01
python3 -m unittest discover -s non-ml/caseload -p 'test_*.py'
```

The command uses only the checked-in manifest. It can write results to a path or print JSON. An explicit `--as-of` changes the event-month cutoff. It does not reconstruct publication-time data vintages.

Each series has a stable local ID, exact service label, facility, locality, region, location level, scope, unit, source and table locator. Source IDs reference the manifest's URL registry. The location level distinguishes facility-wide reporting from a branch, unit, or study cohort. Local IDs identify benchmark series and are independent of identifiers in the routing dataset.

- Use `monthly` for dated counts. Each chunk gives `start` as `YYYY-MM` and one integer or null per consecutive month. A hole in a run is null. A deliberate publication of no recorded activity is zero. When a template displays a calculated zero in an unfilled or future month, retain the underlying missing status. I.T.S Dental College uses that template pattern in calendar 2026. [I.T.S OPD and procedure statistics](https://itsdentalcollege.com/opd-patients)
- Use `period` for a reported total or publisher average. Provide `start` and `end` as inclusive month labels, plus exactly one of `total` or `monthly_average`. A positive integer `exposure_months` can specify a documented denominator for a total. Record an explanation in `notes` if the printed dates or unit need clarification.
- For sourced arithmetic, retain the components or describe the calculation in the notes. The SMIMER delivery series and I.T.S oral surgery series retain their source rows in `transformation` for review. Check that rows are mutually exclusive and use the same months, facility, and denominator before summing them.
- When extending a series, inspect the source scope and period before appending counts. Site openings, scanner downtime, a referral programme change, and a reporting protocol change can all create a level shift. Use a separate series for a changed definition and retain the historical series and dates.
- When refreshing a mutable source page, preserve the previously captured values. Record any revision with its report title, table locator, capture date and scope change. Review an apparent reporting zero against the underlying table before replacing a missing value.

In the output, `first_month` and `last_month` describe the data used. `reported_months` counts numeric months before the cutoff. `months_used` counts individual observations entering the current median. `period_months` is the known denominator for a total expressed monthly. It is null for a publisher mean and for a monthly median. `months_since_last_reported_period` measures the number of closed months between the estimate's last month and the cutoff. `backtest` is present for eligible chronological series and is null otherwise. `targets` records the workload definition and the number of available estimates per Care needed option.

## From a benchmark to load distribution

The site benchmarks establish the scale of delivered activity. They can corroborate that a named reporting site performed a defined service during the report period. Current service eligibility, staffed slots, queues, hours and on-call readiness require separate evidence.

The [healthcare load balancing report](../LOAD_BALANCING_LITERATURE.md) reviews referral and capacity studies and outlines how to evaluate an appointment comparison using this dataset.

The [routine appointment availability audit](ROUTINE_APPOINTMENTS.md) explains the missing booking data, with dialysis as the test case.

The [load evidence bundle](load_evidence.json) feeds the [historical planning comparison](../LOAD_BALANCING.md) and records which capacity values are predicted.

The [Care needed replay report](CARE_OPTIONS_REPLAY.md) tracks caseload and capacity prediction flags independently and explains the outcome for each care option.

1. **Collect comparable observations for the intended facilities.** For each service, record branch ID, site type, role in the referral network, state, district and period alongside the count and its scope. Check source name, branch address, and a stable identifier before joining. Retain source-specific identities and geography until the match is confirmed. A district label describes a reporting location. The patient's home district and referral catchment are separate fields.
2. **Use a clear target for each decision.** Workload is the historical count delivered. To estimate demand presented, add booked, referred, waiting and diverted cases with a definition that avoids double-counting. To estimate pressure, use service-specific capacity in the same unit and period. An example is completed dialysis sessions versus staffed dialysis appointment slots over the same month, with shifts and downtime reflected in the slots. For a bed-limited service, collect bed-days and staffed bed-days or use a validated throughput measure with length of stay.
3. **Validate regional transfer.** For facilities with history, test forecasts on later months. For facilities without history, first build reporting cohorts by activity definition, facility class, referral role and region. Compare a peer median to a pooled count model on held-out facilities, and to a held-out region if regional transfer is a goal. Record counts of facilities and complete months per cohort. Keep separate estimates when a supportable cohort exists, and return unknown when evidence is sparse. Use enough complete months to test seasonality before introducing seasonal terms.
4. **Use dated estimates with matching evidence.** The current pressure input expects facility, service, demand, capacity, unit, period, basis and source URL. Attaching a historical count requires confirmed branch and service identity plus compatible demand and capacity evidence. Retrospective benchmarks can support planning scenarios. Active routing needs current service and travel constraints, especially for urgent options. This baseline runs offline and produces an evidence-backed artifact for analysis. [Pressure input and recommendation code](../src/facility_tool/recommend.py)

For handoff, start with the service definition, observation scope and dates in the JSON. A regional rollup or a pressure ratio is a separate derived quantity with its own coverage requirements.

For regional coverage, summarize both the **number and share** of residents without a verified provider within a chosen travel-time threshold. A small rural catchment can have a high share of residents with limited access. Where address evidence supports only a district or subdistrict, keep the analysis at that geography. For a confirmed location, sum modeled resident counts within a road travel-time catchment and account for overlapping catchments of verified providers. Use current Local Government Directory codes, check older boundaries against census codes, and record the boundaries and population vintage. WorldPop's 2026 India counts are modeled estimates at roughly 100 m resolution. Census 2011 provides recorded population at district, subdistrict, town, village and ward level. [Local Government Directory](https://panchayat.gov.in/lgd/) · [WorldPop 2026 India](https://hub.worldpop.org/geodata/summary?id=73808) · [Census population finder](https://censusindia.gov.in/census.website/data/population-finder)

Maternity is a good first test for population-based workload. Approximate monthly births among catchment residents as population times annual births per 1,000 residents divided by 12,000. Use the NFHS institutional birth share to approximate how many of those births occur at a facility. The SRS birth rates describe states or groups of districts, so transferring them to a smaller catchment is an assumption. These steps estimate institutional births among residents. A facility delivery estimate also needs a birth-to-delivery conversion where relevant, the site's share of local deliveries, and referral flows from outside the catchment. Emergency cesareans and neonatal admissions need their own measured rates. For tertiary services such as cardiac surgery and chemotherapy, referral patterns and comparable provider volumes carry more information than the population immediately surrounding the hospital.
