# Healthcare load balancing across facilities

**Literature review through November 22, 2026.** For the facility finder, the most directly testable use is helping people compare eligible sites for **scheduled care** using a current, bookable service slot and the travel burden. Emergency destination decisions and transfers of admitted patients have different decision makers, time constraints and resource needs. The Indian and international studies below cover all three settings. [Implemented policy and historical replay](LOAD_BALANCING.md)

## Experience in India

| Study or programme | Evidence and relevance |
| --- | --- |
| **Puducherry maternity referral, 2025.** Dorairajan et al., *BMJ Global Health*. | In a JIPMER quality-improvement initiative, a dedicated team assessed antenatal risk, referred suitable lower-risk patients to nearby services, and communicated with receiving facilities. JIPMER births declined from 18,140 in 2019 to 10,542 in 2023, and reported antenatal bed occupancy fell from 130–150% to 85–90%. Births were also 26% lower than the post-restriction, pre-triage volume. These are before-and-after observations at the tertiary site. The study does not quantify the effect on receiving-site workload across the network. [Study and figures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12742052/) |
| **Tamil Nadu STEMI network, 2025.** Gnanaraj et al., *Indian Journal of Medical Research*. | Eighteen hubs linked to 188 spoke hospitals used ECG teleconsultation and coordinated referrals. Among 71,907 cases recorded in 2019–23, guideline-based revascularisation rose from 69.6% to 75.8%. This is a programme trend with concurrent changes in services. The operational lesson is to represent distinct facility roles, referral links and receiving capability for a time-critical pathway. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40257139/) · [Tamil Nadu programme](https://nhm.tn.gov.in/tn-heart-attack-management-program/) |
| **Referral choices in southwest Delhi, 2025.** Fatma et al., *BMC Health Services Research*. | A survey at 22 public primary and secondary sites included 449 patients. About 59% said they would consider a same-tier referral to a less congested site. Consistent quality of care ranked ahead of waiting and travel time. The survey measured stated preferences in one district, where providers also raised concerns about digital access. [Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-025-13602-y) |
| **Ambulance activity in Punjab and Haryana, 2026.** Moonis et al., *Public Health Action*. | A descriptive study of 626,372 hotline calls in 2025 found that 61% of trips that used the service were interfacility transfers. The share delivered to a facility within 60 minutes of the call was 50% in Punjab and 74% in Haryana. The study describes baseline workflows and timing; it tests no alternative routing policy. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42830954/) |
| **Stroke access in India, 2025.** Asif et al., *International Journal of Stroke*. | Using March 2021 facility data, 2020 population projections and driving times, the study estimated that 26.3% of the population was within an hour of an intravenous thrombolysis-capable centre and 20.6% of an endovascular treatment-capable centre. These are modelled historical access estimates. Capability-specific catchments offer a useful way to identify areas with few viable alternatives. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39719435/) |
| **Dialysis and travel, 2026.** Ramasamy et al., *Cost Effectiveness and Resource Allocation*, and the national dialysis programme. | Among 99 maintenance-dialysis patients studied at AIIMS Raipur in 2021, the median distance travelled was 30 km, with substantial costs beyond treatment coverage. The national programme documents checking vacancies and scheduling sessions, which gives a concrete example of the operational data a pilot would need. Access to data and update quality for a facility-finder integration remain open. [Raipur study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12962-026-00753-w) · [NHM training module](https://nhm.gov.in/New-Update-2025-26/Whats-new/NCD-Medical-Officers.pdf) · [August 2026 parliamentary committee report summary](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2296281&lang=2&reg=48) |

India's 2026 National Ambulance Services guidelines call for coordinated dispatch, structured triage, GPS and mapping of referral facilities, bed availability and critical-care readiness. They describe the national framework. Actual feed coverage, freshness and receiving arrangements need to be established locally. [Ministry announcement, June 29, 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2279073&lang=1&reg=3)

Population is useful for estimating how many people live within reach of an eligible provider, or how many have no plausible alternative. The India stroke study illustrates why the service definition belongs in that calculation. For demand planning, population can enter a catchment scenario alongside observed referrals and service use. A facility's local population alone leaves referral inflow, ability to pay, unmet need and current slots unknown. [India stroke accessibility study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39719435/) · [Delhi referral study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-025-13602-y) · [Granular rural primary-care access working paper](https://iegindia.org/working-paper/granular-evaluation-of-public-primary-healthcare-accessibility-in-rural-india/)

## What happened in operational studies

| Decision | Observed evidence and limits |
| --- | --- |
| **Transfer eligible admitted patients to affiliate hospitals.** Xu et al., *Journal of Hospital Medicine*, 2026. | In an Oregon health system, a pre/post programme moved 537 patients. Transfers increased from 1.5 to 15 per week. Among transferred patients, ED boarding declined from 6.5 to 2.9 hours across improvement cycles. The intervention combined screening, patient communication, a receiving unit, dedicated transport and a shared workflow. Selection and concurrent changes limit causal interpretation. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42768806/) · [Author abstract](https://ohsu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/level-loading-across-a-health-system-a-collaborative-model-for-in/) |
| **Coordinate transfer requests across hospitals.** *JAMA Network Open*, 2025. | An interrupted time series covering 441,709 transfers across eight US states found an immediate increase after pandemic coordination centres started (rate ratio 1.35, 95% CI 1.05–1.74), followed by a longer-term decrease in transfer rates (rate ratio 0.94, 95% CI 0.90–0.97). More transfers measure coordination activity. The study does not determine effects on time to treatment or patient outcomes. [Study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2842106) |
| **Use the dispatch-assigned, usually nearest, destination.** Youssef et al., *American Journal of Emergency Medicine*, 2026. | A before-and-after study of 943,658 ED visits in New York City's public hospital network found that geometric mean time to clinician fell from 15 to 12 minutes after the policy shift. ED length of stay changed from 151 to 149 minutes. The study provides a benchmark for nearest-eligible routing in one network. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42269317/) |
| **Coordinate ambulance diversion through EMS.** Farah et al., *Prehospital Emergency Care*, 2024. | A retrospective comparison of three 27-day periods covering 50,992 San Diego County transports found that the median of daily 90th-percentile offload time fell from 53.9 to 40.3 minutes during county-led diversion compared with hospital-led diversion. The county-led period also had fewer transports per day, limiting attribution. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38776421/) |
| **Use crowding status to predict ambulance handover.** Mol et al., *International Journal of Emergency Medicine*, 2025. | At two EDs of one Dutch hospital, 2,967 ambulance arrivals showed no statistically significant association between a traffic-light crowding status and offload-delay categories (p = 0.109). The signal needs local validation against the delay it is meant to predict. [Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12245-025-01068-y) |
| **Accept a referral at a crowded tertiary centre.** Greenwood-Ericksen et al., *JAMA Network Open*, 2025. | At one referral centre, 61.7% of 26,020 requests were accepted. The highest ED-boarding quartile had lower adjusted odds of acceptance than the lowest (0.71, 95% CI 0.66–0.78). Overall rural-origin requests also had lower adjusted odds of acceptance than urban-origin requests. Record actual acceptance and measure outcomes by origin and referral type. [Study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2834521) |
| **Choose the appropriate endpoint for suspected stroke.** TRIAGE-STROKE, *Stroke*, 2023. | In this under-recruited Danish randomized trial, direct transport to the comprehensive centre shortened time to thrombectomy puncture by 35 minutes in patients with large-vessel occlusion. The primary-centre route shortened time to thrombolysis by 30 minutes. The study did not resolve the difference in 90-day functional outcomes. The relevant endpoint is time to the indicated treatment under the clinical pathway. [Trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10589426/) |

## Methods tested in models

| Method and study | What was tested |
| --- | --- |
| **Queueing network for acute wards and ICUs.** Chan et al., *Manufacturing & Service Operations Management*, 2026. | With data from 17 Ontario hospitals, the model estimated up to 48.4% fewer ward and 26.4% fewer ICU patient-days above 95% capacity compared with no transfers. Optimizing disease-specific distribution alone could worsen all-patient overoccupancy. The gains are simulated and the threshold belongs to the study. [Study](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2025.0497) |
| **Robust optimisation for surge capacity.** Parker et al., *Health Care Management Science*, 2026. | A retrospective Johns Hopkins case estimated that joint bed deployment and 32 transfers over 63 days could reduce surge capacity needs by nearly 90%. Setup times, transfer restrictions and uncertain arrivals were model inputs. The estimated gain comes from the counterfactual, and the code is available for adapting the formulation. [Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10729-026-09764-8) · [Code](https://github.com/flixpar/hospital-capacity-management-optimization) |
| **Pool scheduled referrals and treatment slots.** Seyedi et al., *CMAJ*, 2025. | A discrete-event simulation of 17,465 Ontario hip and knee procedures found that pooling referrals improved the longest waits for consultation. Pooling consultations alone increased median and 90th-percentile waits for surgery in three of five regions. Pooling both stages improved the 90th percentile of total wait in all five. Measure time to completed care and track whether a shorter first queue shifts delay downstream. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12109095/) |
| **Estimate patient time at arrival.** Fatma and Ramamohan, *OR Spectrum*, 2023, and 2026 preprint. | A nine-facility simulation for childbirth and inpatient care found that the distribution of delays depended on forecast accuracy. A subsequent two-PHC model compared nearest assignment with travel plus predicted in-facility time at arrival. The latter used queueing and simulation-trained machine learning and tested different levels of adoption. Both studies evaluate a policy in simulation. [Peer-reviewed study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00291-022-00704-w) · [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07921v1) |
| **Publish predicted waits.** Zou et al., *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association*, 2026. | A simulation calibrated to three Hong Kong EDs varied forecast quality, update frequency and adoption. Accurate, frequently refreshed predictions improved modelled outcomes under adoption. Inaccurate predictions at high adoption could make congestion worse. Test what happens as people act on a displayed recommendation. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41911388/) |

## Implications for the facility finder

The operational selector starts with facility eligibility and compares fresh, measured service availability within detour limits. The historical planning selector uses workload divided by equivalent capacity for a specified period and basis. Both Streamlit and the notebook lookup now use the measured comparison for routine searches. Their travel inputs differ. [Implemented policy](LOAD_BALANCING.md) · [Scenario notebook](notebooks/08_load_policy_scenarios.ipynb)

The caseload audit gives historical volume at 26 matched facilities. Use those counts to calibrate planning scenarios by care option and location. A recent monthly count can represent throughput under the conditions of that report, and activity that the service never received or completed can be missing. For scheduled load balancing, collect a same-service measure that a patient can act on, such as the next bookable slot, its eligibility and prerequisites, and how recently it was confirmed. For bed-limited planning, use staffed beds or staffed bed-days with matching census or demand and length of stay. A current queue or staffing estimate needs its own source. [Project caseload audit](caseload/EXPANSION.md) · [PMNDP mobile manual](https://pmndp.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/mobile_app_user_manual.pdf) · [Queueing network study](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2025.0497)

An experiment informed by this evidence would start with **one scheduled service and a cooperating set of facilities**. Dialysis is a candidate if the programme can supply comparable recurring slots and each patient can maintain the prescribed schedule. Routine MRI is another candidate where centres can confirm appointments for the same exam protocol. Offer the nearest eligible option and a confirmed lower-wait alternative inside the person's travel and cost limits. Treat missing or stale availability as unknown, preserve patient choice, and limit emergency use to existing clinically governed pathways. This is a design inference from the studies. [Dialysis travel study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12962-026-00753-w) · [Imaging patient-choice experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12084043/) · [Tamil Nadu STEMI programme](https://nhm.tn.gov.in/tn-heart-attack-management-program/)

The follow-up audit found no usable public dialysis vacancy data for this experiment. [Routine appointment availability audit](caseload/ROUTINE_APPOINTMENTS.md)

### Evaluate against the current ranking

1. Replay the same requests under **nearest eligible**, **current opt-in weighting**, and **earliest confirmed appropriate appointment within the travel limit**. Keep the service eligibility rules constant. Record the eligible alternatives at decision time, the data age, the displayed option, and any decision to decline it.
2. Measure median and 90th-percentile request-to-completed-care time, added travel by actual transport mode, booking and completion rates, no-shows or missed recurring sessions, refusal or onward referral, and the count of feasible alternatives. Measure peak load at receiving sites and across the network. Report outcomes by service, geography and rurality, with travel and out-of-pocket costs where available.
3. Test stale and missing feeds, demand surges at several sites, closures or reduced staffing, and different levels of patient uptake. Run in shadow mode with decision-time snapshots before a prospective evaluation. A replay shows how the recommendation would change. The patient's actual outcome at the alternative site requires a prospective comparison or an explicit simulation with validated assumptions. Set thresholds using local travel and clinical constraints. [Ontario planned-care simulation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12109095/) · [Effect of publishing waits](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41911388/) · [Rural and urban referral acceptance](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2834521)

## Review scope

This targeted review prioritised original studies and Indian programme materials, with papers published in 2023–26. Searches covered PubMed, publishers, scholarly and web indexes, and Indian government sources. Terms included *hospital load balancing*, *level-loading*, *interhospital transfer*, *ambulance destination*, *patient referral overcrowding India*, *appointment pooling*, and *queueing facility assignment*. Outcomes from implemented interventions, outcomes modelled against a counterfactual, and preferences or coverage estimates answer different questions. The evidence reviewed here supports testing a service-specific facility comparison. The magnitude of benefit in India remains an empirical question for that service and its participating sites.
